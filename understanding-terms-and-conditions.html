Understanding Terms & Conditions

Don't click "I Agree" blindly — know what you're signing up for

Raise your hand if you've ever clicked "I Agree" without reading a single word. Be honest! We all do it. It's just a long block of text that nobody has time to read, right? But here's the scary part: when you click "I Agree," you might be giving companies permission to collect your photos, track your location, share your data with advertisers, or even sell your information to third parties without you ever knowing. In 2022, a major social media company was fined €1.2 billion for transferring user data without proper consent. In 2023, a popular fitness app was caught selling users' location data to insurance companies. These legal documents are written in complicated language for a reason — but today, we're going to decode them. You'll learn what to look for, what to watch out for, and how to protect your rights. You'll never click "I Agree" blindly again.

📚 What You Need to Know

What Are Terms & Conditions?
Terms & Conditions (also called Terms of Service or Terms of Use) is a legal agreement between you (the user) and the company that provides the service. It outlines the rules, rights, and responsibilities of both parties. When you create an account or use a service, you are entering into a legally binding contract with that company.
What T&C Typically Cover:
Section What It Includes
Acceptance of Terms By using the service, you agree to follow the rules
User Accounts How to create accounts, password requirements, age limits
User Conduct What you can and cannot do on the platform
Content Ownership Who owns the content you post
Data Collection & Privacy What data is collected and how it's used
Third-Party Services Links to other websites or services
Termination When and how your account can be suspended or deleted
Disclaimers & Liability What the company is not responsible for
Dispute Resolution How legal disputes are handled
Real-World Example: Instagram's T&C state that you grant them a license to use your content (photos, videos, etc.) so they can display it on their platform. However, you still own your content. This license allows Instagram to show your posts to other users and use them for platform features like sharing and embedding.
Key Clauses to Look For
Not all T&C are created equal. Some are fair and transparent; others are designed to take advantage of users. There are specific clauses you should always look for before agreeing.
Critical Clauses You Should Always Check:
Clause What It Means Questions to Ask
Data Collection What data does the company collect? Do they collect location, contacts, messages, photos?
Data Usage How will your data be used? Will they use it for advertising, research, selling?
Data Sharing Will your data be shared with third parties? Who are they sharing it with? Can you opt out?
User Rights What rights do you have over your content? Do you own your content? Can you delete it?
Termination Can the company suspend or delete your account? Under what circumstances? Can you appeal?
Age Requirements What is the minimum age to use the service? Are there different rules for different regions?
Dispute Resolution How are legal disputes handled? Can you sue? Do you have to use arbitration?
Changes to Terms Can the company change the T&C without notice? Will they notify you of changes?
Real-World Example: A gaming app's T&C may state that they collect your device ID, location, contacts, and in-app behavior. If you don't agree, you can't use the app. Many users click "I Agree" without realizing they are giving the company access to their contacts and location.
Age Restrictions
Many platforms require users to be at least 13 years old (under COPPA in the US) or 16 years old (under GDPR in Europe). These age restrictions exist to protect children from inappropriate content, data collection, and online predators.
Why Age Restrictions Exist:
Reason Explanation
Child Protection Protects children from predators and harmful content
Data Privacy Children's data is protected under laws like COPPA and GDPR
Age-Appropriate Content Ensures users see content suitable for their age
Legal Compliance Companies must comply with laws to avoid massive fines
Parental Consent For underage users, parental consent is often required
Age Requirements by Platform:
Platform Minimum Age Region
Instagram 13 Worldwide (under COPPA)
TikTok 13 13+ in US, 16+ in some regions
Facebook 13 Worldwide (under COPPA)
Snapchat 13 Worldwide
YouTube 13 13+ for full features
Twitter/X 13 Worldwide
WhatsApp 13 16+ in Europe under GDPR
Fortnite 13 With parental consent for younger
ChatGPT 13 18+ in some regions
Why You Shouldn't Lie About Your Age:
  • ❌ It violates the Terms of Service — your account could be suspended or permanently banned
  • ❌ You'll see content not meant for your age group (potentially harmful)
  • ❌ Your data may be used in ways you don't understand or consent to
  • ❌ You miss out on safety features designed specifically for younger users
  • ❌ The company could face legal consequences for collecting data from underage users
Real-World Example: A 12-year-old student signs up for TikTok by entering a fake birth year. They are exposed to content that is inappropriate for their age — including challenges, adult humor, and potentially dangerous trends. Additionally, their data is being collected and used in ways that violate TikTok's actual Terms of Service. If caught, the account would be permanently banned.
Data Collection — What They Take and Why
When you use a service, the company collects data about you. This data can range from basic information like your email address to detailed information like your location, browsing history, contacts, and even your biometric data.
Types of Data Collected by Platforms:
Data Type Examples Why Companies Collect It
Personal Identifiers Name, email, phone number, date of birth To identify you, send notifications, verify identity
Device Information Device type, operating system, IP address To optimize the app, track usage, prevent fraud
Location Data GPS location, IP location, Wi-Fi networks For location-based features, targeted ads
Usage Data What you click, how long you use the app, search history To improve the product, personalize content
Contacts Your phone contacts, friends lists To connect you with friends, suggest followers
Content Photos, videos, messages, posts you create To display your content, analyze trends
Payment Information Credit card numbers, purchase history To process transactions, prevent fraud
How Companies Use Your Data:
Purpose Explanation
Targeted Advertising Show ads based on your interests, location, and behavior
Product Improvement Analyze how people use the app to make it better
Personalization Show content and recommendations tailored to you
Security Detect suspicious activity and prevent fraud
Sharing with Third Parties Sell or share data with partners and advertisers
Legal Compliance Comply with laws and regulations
Research Conduct market research and develop new products
Real-World Example: A fitness app collects location data (GPS tracking) during runs and workouts. The app then sells this data to advertisers who target users with ads for sports gear, gyms, and health products. In some cases, the data is also sold to insurance companies who use it to adjust premiums based on how active users are.
Termination Rights — When Your Account Can Be Banned
Termination rights allow the company to suspend or permanently delete your account if you violate the Terms of Service. This is a powerful tool that companies use to enforce their rules.
Reasons Your Account Can Be Terminated:
Reason Example
Violating T&C Posting prohibited content, using fake information
Harassment/Bullying Sending threatening messages, cyberbullying
Illegal Activity Using the platform for illegal purposes
Impersonation Pretending to be someone else
Spam/Scams Sending unsolicited messages, phishing
Hate Speech Posting discriminatory or offensive content
Copyright Infringement Sharing copyrighted material without permission
Inactivity Some platforms delete inactive accounts after a certain period
What Happens When You're Banned:
Stage What Happens
Warning You may receive a warning for minor violations
Temporary Suspension Your account is temporarily locked (hours/days/weeks)
Permanent Ban Your account is permanently deleted and cannot be recovered
IP/Device Ban Your IP address or device is blocked from creating new accounts
Legal Action In severe cases, the company may take legal action
Real-World Example: A student posts offensive content targeting a classmate on Instagram. The post is reported, and Instagram's moderation team reviews it. The student's account is permanently banned, and they lose all their photos, messages, and followers. The student cannot create a new account using the same email or phone number.
Your Rights — What You Can Do
Despite all the data collection and rules, you still have rights as a user. Many countries have laws that protect your digital rights.
Key User Rights:
Right What It Means
Right to Access You can request a copy of all data the company has collected about you
Right to Rectification You can correct inaccurate data
Right to Erasure You can request that your data be deleted ("Right to be Forgotten")
Right to Restrict Processing You can limit how your data is used
Right to Data Portability You can transfer your data to another service
Right to Object You can object to certain uses of your data (like targeted ads)
Right to Withdraw Consent You can withdraw your consent at any time
Right to Complain You can file a complaint with a data protection authority
How to Exercise Your Rights:
Platform Where to Go
Google/Gmail Takeout.google.com (download data)
Facebook/Instagram Settings → Your Information → Download Your Information
TikTok Settings → Privacy → Download your data
Twitter/X Settings → Your Account → Download an archive of your data
Apple Privacy.apple.com (manage data)
Real-World Example: A student realizes they want to leave Instagram. They go to Settings → Your Information → Download Your Information and request a copy of all their data. The platform sends them a file containing their posts, messages, photos, and activity history. They then delete their account.

✅ Examples for Students

Example 1 — Instagram's T&C Highlights
Category What Instagram Does
Data Collected Your device info, IP address, location, photos, videos, messages, search history, and even what you browse outside the app
How Data Is Used Targeted ads, content recommendations, platform improvement, showing posts to others
Who Data Is Shared With Parent company (Meta), advertisers, third-party partners, and affiliates
Your Rights You own your content, but you grant Instagram a license to display, distribute, and modify it
Age Requirement 13+ (with parental consent for 13-18 in some regions)
Termination Instagram can suspend or delete your account if you violate T&C
Example 2 — Fortnite's T&C Highlights
Category What Fortnite Does
Data Collected In-game actions, voice chat audio (in some regions), device info, IP address, purchase history
How Data Is Used Game improvement, anti-cheat systems, personalized experiences
Who Data Is Shared With Partners, affiliates, and third-party service providers
Your Rights You can request data deletion in some regions
Age Requirement 13+ (with parental consent for younger players)
Termination You can be banned for cheating, offensive language, or chargebacks
Example 3 — ChatGPT's T&C Highlights
Category What ChatGPT Does
Data Collected Conversations, usage data, IP address, device info
How Data Is Used To improve the AI model, provide better responses, and for research
Who Data Is Shared With Third-party service providers for hosting and analysis (not with advertisers)
Your Rights You can opt out of having your conversations used for training (in settings)
Age Requirement 13+ (18+ in some regions)
Termination Accounts can be banned for misuse, spam, or violating usage policies
Example 4 — Comparing T&C: What to Look For
Clause Why It Matters Real-World Example
Data Collection You need to know what they're taking from you A photo app collects your location, contacts, AND photos — that's a lot!
Data Sharing Your data might end up with people you don't know A free game sells your data to advertisers who target you with ads
Termination Your account can disappear without warning A student's account is banned and they lose 3 years of posts
Age Requirement Don't lie about your age — you'll see age-inappropriate content A 12-year-old sees adult content because they pretended to be 16
Dispute Resolution You may not be able to sue the company Many apps require you to use arbitration instead of going to court

🧪 Activity: T&C Scavenger Hunt

Part 1: Research
  • Students are divided into 3 groups. Each group is assigned a platform:
    Group A: Instagram  |  Group B: TikTok  |  Group C: YouTube
  • Each group must find:
    1. Minimum age to use the platform
    2. 3 types of data the platform collects
    3. 1 thing users are forbidden to do
    4. What happens if T&C are violated
    5. Can users delete their data?
    6. Does the platform share data with third parties?
    7. What rights do users have over their content?
