Lesson 06

Digital Footprint & Reputation

Your online presence matters — manage it wisely

Imagine if everything you've ever posted online — every photo, every comment, every like, and every meme — was printed out and given to your future boss, teacher, or college admissions officer. Would you be proud or embarrassed? Your digital footprint is like a permanent tattoo that follows you forever. A student in the US lost a university scholarship because of an old tweet using inappropriate language. In Thailand, employers increasingly check applicants' social media before hiring. But don't worry — it's not too late. Today, you'll learn to audit your digital footprint and build a positive online reputation that opens doors instead of closing them.

📚 What You Need to Know

What is a Digital Footprint?
A digital footprint is all the data you leave behind when you use the internet. Every time you post, search, click, like, or comment, you're adding to your digital footprint.
Active vs. Passive Footprint:
TypeWhat It IsExamples
Active FootprintData you consciously sharePosts, comments, photos, videos, messages
Passive FootprintData collected about you without your knowledgeLocation data, browsing history, search history, IP address
Where Your Digital Footprint Comes From:
  • 📱 Social Media — Posts, comments, likes, shares, profile information
  • 🔍 Search Engines — Search history, clicked links
  • 🌐 Websites — Browsing history, cookies, IP address
  • 📲 Apps — Location data, usage patterns, device info
Real-World Example: A student visits a travel website, searches for flights, and looks at hotels. Later, they see ads for flights and hotels on their social media feed. This is because their passive footprint was collected and used for targeted advertising.
Why Your Digital Footprint Matters
Your digital footprint is like a permanent record of your online activity. It can affect your future in many ways.
Who Might Check Your Digital Footprint:
WhoWhat They Look ForWhy It Matters
College AdmissionsPosts, comments, online behaviorOne negative post can cost you admission
EmployersProfessionalism, judgment, values70% of employers check social media before hiring
Scholarship CommitteesCharacter, behaviorScholarships can be revoked for inappropriate posts
Law EnforcementEvidence of illegal activityPosts can be used in court
Real-World Example: A student posts a photo holding a drink at a party. The photo is shared and seen by a university admissions officer. The student's application is rejected because the university has a strict anti-drinking policy. The student didn't even drink the alcohol — but the photo created the wrong impression.
Managing Your Digital Footprint
You can't delete your digital footprint entirely, but you can manage it. Here's how to clean up and control your online presence.
Steps to Manage Your Digital Footprint:
StepActionWhy It's Important
1Google YourselfSee what others see when they search your name
2Delete Old PostsRemove embarrassing or unprofessional content
3Adjust Privacy SettingsLimit who can see your posts
4Post PositivelyShare content you'd be proud of
5Be Kind OnlineYour comments reflect your character
6Create a Positive PresenceLinkedIn, portfolio, achievements
What to Post Instead:
  • ✅ Achievements — Shows hard work and success
  • ✅ Volunteer work — Shows kindness and community involvement
  • ✅ Hobbies and interests — Shows personality and passion
  • ✅ Positive comments — Shows kindness and respect
Real-World Example: A student graduates high school and applies to university. Before applying, they: (1) Google themselves and find an old blog post with embarrassing content, (2) Delete the blog post, (3) Set their Instagram to Private, (4) Remove 5 unprofessional tweets, (5) Create a LinkedIn profile with their achievements. The university searches their name and finds a positive, professional presence. The student is accepted.
The "Grandmother Test" Before you post anything, ask yourself: "Would I be comfortable if my grandmother, my teacher, or my future employer saw this?"
If the answer is no, don't post it.
