Lesson 07

Cyberbullying Awareness

Stand up, speak out, stop bullying

Words can hurt more than fists. In the digital world, bullying follows you home. It happens in group chats, on social media, and even in online games. In Thailand, a recent study showed that 1 in 3 teenagers have experienced cyberbullying — and many didn't know how to respond. The victims often feel trapped, scared, and alone. In some tragic cases, cyberbullying has led to self-harm and suicide. But here's the truth — you have power. You can block, report, and most importantly, support others. Today, we're going to build a classroom culture where everyone feels safe — online and offline. You have the power to stop cyberbullying.

📚 What You Need to Know

What is Cyberbullying?
Cyberbullying is using digital platforms to harass, threaten, embarrass, or target another person. It can happen anywhere people communicate online — social media, messaging apps, email, online games, and more.
Types of Cyberbullying:
TypeWhat It IsExample
HarassmentRepeatedly sending mean, offensive, or threatening messagesSomeone sends 20 mean messages in a row
ImpersonationPretending to be someone else to embarrass themCreating a fake Instagram account using a classmate's name and photos
DoxingSharing someone's personal information without permissionPosting someone's address and phone number online
ExclusionIntentionally leaving someone out of online groupsRemoving a student from the class group chat
RumorsSpreading lies or gossip onlineSaying someone cheated on a test when they didn't
TrollingPosting provocative messages to upset someoneLeaving mean comments on someone's photos
Where Cyberbullying Happens:
  • 📱 Social Media — Mean comments, fake accounts, sharing embarrassing posts
  • 💬 Messaging Apps — Private messages, group chats, threatening texts
  • 🎮 Online Games — Harassment in chat, deliberately ruining gameplay
Real-World Example: A student is excluded from a class group chat and receives mean messages calling them "stupid." The messages are sent by someone who created a fake account. The victim feels isolated and scared.
The Impact of Cyberbullying
Cyberbullying can have serious effects on victims. It's not just "words on a screen" — it's real harm to real people.
Effects on Victims:
EffectSigns
DepressionWithdrawing, sadness, loss of interest
AnxietyFear, worry, panic attacks
Low Self-EsteemFeeling worthless, negative self-talk
Academic IssuesFalling grades, skipping school
Physical SymptomsHeadaches, stomachaches, trouble sleeping
Social WithdrawalAvoiding friends and activities
Self-HarmCutting, eating disorders, risky behavior
Suicidal ThoughtsTalking about suicide, giving away belongings
Real-World Example: A student is cyberbullied for 6 months. They stop going to school, stop talking to friends, and become depressed. Their grades drop from A's to F's. They start having panic attacks and trouble sleeping. They feel completely alone. This is a real consequence of cyberbullying.
How to Respond to Cyberbullying
If you're being cyberbullied, there are specific steps you should take. Don't suffer in silence — take action.
Step-by-Step Response:
StepActionWhy It's Important
1Don't Respond EmotionallyResponding can escalate the situation
2Don't Delete MessagesKeep evidence for reporting
3Take ScreenshotsSave evidence before deleting anything
4Block the UserPrevents further contact
5Report to the PlatformThe platform can take action (ban the user, remove content)
6Tell a Trusted AdultParents, teacher, school counselor
7Report to SchoolIf it involves classmates, the school can intervene
8Contact AuthoritiesIf it's a threat of violence or illegal
Reporting on Popular Platforms:
PlatformHow to Report
InstagramTap ... → Report → Choose reason
TikTokTap Share → Report → Choose reason
FacebookTap ... → Find Support or Report
SnapchatPress and hold → Report → Choose reason
Real-World Example: A student receives a threatening DM on Instagram: "I know where you live. I'm going to come to your school tomorrow." They DON'T reply, DON'T delete the message, take a screenshot, report the message to Instagram, block the user, tell their parents and teacher, and the school counselor is informed.
How to Be a Good Bystander
If you see cyberbullying happening to someone else, you are a bystander. You have the power to help. Don't just watch — take action.
What to Do as a Bystander:
  • 💬 Support the Victim — Send a private message: "I'm sorry you're going through this. Are you okay?"
  • 🚫 Don't Engage the Bully — Don't respond — it gives them attention
  • 🚩 Report the Bullying — Report the content to the platform
  • 👨‍🏫 Tell a Trusted Adult — Tell a teacher, parent, or school counselor
  • 🗣️ Speak Up — Say: "That's not okay. Please stop."
Real-World Example: A student sees their classmate being bullied in a group chat. The bully says: "You're so ugly. No one likes you." The bystander says: "That's not true. Please stop." They then start a new group chat with the victim and other friends to make sure they feel included. This simple act of kindness can make a huge difference.
Remember: If you see something, say something.
You have the power to stop cyberbullying and support those who are hurting.

✅ Examples for Students

Example 1 — Cyberbullying Scenarios
ScenarioWhat to Do
Harassment: Someone comments "You're so ugly 😂" with 50 likesDon't reply. Screenshot. Report. Block. Tell a trusted adult.
Impersonation: Someone creates a fake TikTok account using your name and photosScreenshot everything. Report the account. Tell friends to report it too. Tell a trusted adult.
Exclusion: You're removed from a group chat and ignoredTalk to friends about it. Don't respond to bullying. Tell a trusted adult. Find other friends to chat with.
Example 2 — Bystander in Action

Scenario: A group chat is making fun of a classmate for being poor.

Bystander Response:

  1. Say: "That's not funny. Please stop."
  2. Privately message the victim: "I'm sorry you had to see that. Are you okay?"
  3. Report the chat to the teacher.
  4. Start a new group chat with the victim and other friends.

🧪 Activity: Role-Play Responding to Cyberbullying

Let's Get Started!
Part 1: Scenario Setup
  • Students are divided into groups of 3: Victim, Bully, Bystander
  • Each group receives a cyberbullying scenario
Part 2: Role-Play
  • Students act out the scenario
  • The victim responds appropriately (doesn't reply, takes screenshots, reports, tells adult)
  • The bystander intervenes
  • The bully is confronted
Part 3: Reflection & Discussion
  • "How did it feel to be the victim?"
  • "How did it feel to be the bystander?"
  • "What would you do differently?"
  • "Why do you think people cyberbully?"
  • "What can we do to prevent cyberbullying in our school?"
  • "How can we support victims of cyberbullying?"
